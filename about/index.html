About By-the-Book Workplace Investigations

We built the investigation practice we once needed.

An investigation is not just a case file. It's someone who has come forward with a concern, someone being asked to respond, leaders trying to make responsible decisions, and an internal team trying to keep the process fair while the organization continues to operate.

We understand that responsibility because we have carried it from the inside. By-the-Book exists to provide the outside support that internal teams can genuinely rely on.

The experience that changed how we work

Before founding By-the-Book, our founder led an internal investigations unit. A high-profile sexual harassment matter needed outside neutrality, so the organization retained a third-party investigation firm. The expectation was straightforward: an objective process, a timely report, and findings everyone involved could trust had been reached fairly.

The final report came about eight weeks later than expected and over budget. More troubling, from the internal team's perspective, it read more like a legal defense of the organization than an impartial examination of the facts. It did not adequately account for the lived experiences of either the complainant or the respondent.

Leadership was dissatisfied. So were the parties. The organization had to revisit work it believed would be finished, adding expense, delay, and strain to a workplace already feeling the effects of the complaint.

The outside investigator was supposed to relieve a difficult situation. Instead, the internal team was left managing the consequences of an investigation that had not delivered what the organization needed.

That experience became the reason for By-the-Book.

We believed an outside investigation should be independent without being disconnected, legally and analytically rigorous without becoming advocacy, and attentive to people without prejudging the facts.

What we know from the inside

When you lead an investigation function, you cannot look at a case in isolation. You have to think about what your policy requires, what the timeline allows, who should and should not be involved, what the parties have been told, and whether the eventual findings will give decision-makers a clear foundation for their next steps.

You also know what happens when the process drifts. A case sits while a team is short. The organization loses confidence. The people involved are left waiting. And the person who engaged the outside firm is the one answering the questions.

That is why we pay attention to the details that can be overlooked from the outside: scope, realistic milestones, communication, evidentiary discipline, policy application, and how the report will function once it leaves our hands.

What a fair investigation owes everyone

  • To the complainant, it owes a serious, respectful assessment of the concerns raised, without assuming every allegation is established.

  • To the respondent, it owes a meaningful opportunity to respond and a fair evaluation of the evidence, without assuming wrongdoing.

  • To the organization, it owes findings grounded in the record and the relevant standards, explained clearly enough to support an informed decision.

  • To the investigator, it owes a process worth standing behind. The person doing this work needs structure, support, and the confidence that the method holds up when the case gets hard. That is the gap By-the-Book was built to close.

And to everyone involved, it owes a process that does not confuse neutrality with indifference.

We do not investigate to produce a preferred answer. We investigate to determine what the evidence supports, what it does not, and what still cannot be resolved.

How we put that into practice

Every engagement follows the CLEAR By-the-Book™ Investigation Framework: Capture the Complaint, Launch the Strategy, Evaluate the Evidence, Analyze Impartially, and Report with Clarity.

C L E A R

Our work combines protected-class and policy analysis with the practical experience of leading internal teams. We are deliberate about scope and communication, careful with the evidence, and direct in our reporting. We distinguish our factual findings from the decisions an organization is responsible for making.

The goal is a process the parties can recognize as fair and a report leadership can understand without having to translate it from legal shorthand.

Third-Party Protected-Class Investigations

For cases your team cannot take on, or should not handle internally.

You may be between hires, dealing with more cases than your team can reasonably absorb, or facing an allegation that involves someone in your own reporting structure. We can handle a defined investigation or help with a continuing caseload.

Our practice focuses on protected-class discrimination, harassment, including sexual harassment, retaliation involving legally protected activity, and related disability accommodation concerns. We do not offer general employee-relations investigations unrelated to protected-class issues.

Whether we handle one case or several, our role is impartial fact-finding. We clarify the allegations, review the evidence, apply the relevant policies and legal standards, and explain what the record supports. Your organization makes the decisions that follow.

  1. 01

    Investigation Capacity Retainer

    For the cases that keep coming while your team is stretched.

    A vacancy could take months to fill. A leadership transition has slowed assignments. Or your team is working through a backlog while new complaints continue to arrive. You may absolutely need to hire. The cases on your desk still need attention in the meantime.

    The Investigation Capacity Retainer gives you an agreed bank of hours for recurring protected-class investigations. Rather than starting a new outside search each time, we establish a way to refer and scope the work, confirm capacity, and communicate throughout an engagement.

    Depending on the arrangement, that can include:

    • A defined bank of hours and a clear case-referral process.
    • Agreed assignment, response, and communication expectations.
    • A designated lead, defined scope, milestones, and updates on hours used.
    • A way to reassess needs and available capacity as the caseload changes.

    Your team remains responsible for its investigation function. We take on the cases covered by the engagement and keep you informed as the work moves forward.

  2. 02

    Single-Case Investigation

    For a case that needs to be handled outside your team.

    Sometimes the issue is not the overall caseload. It is one case. Maybe it involves a senior executive, someone in your investigation team's reporting line, a potential conflict of interest, or likely public scrutiny. Or maybe it is simply a case your team cannot absorb right now.

    We take on a defined protected-class investigation without an ongoing commitment. Before work begins, we discuss possible conflicts, clarify the allegations and scope, agree on reasonable milestones, and establish the fee and communication arrangements.

    Our report sets out the evidence, applicable standards, analysis, and findings so your decision-makers have a clear basis for their next steps.

What to expect with either arrangement

We agree on scope and communication expectations before the work begins. If something materially changes the timeline or cost, we talk with you about it. Our findings stay grounded in the evidence and the standards that apply; the decisions that follow remain yours.

Investigation Readiness Intensive

Your best investigator should not be your entire investigation strategy.

Great investigators matter. Their judgment, experience, and ability to navigate difficult cases are invaluable. But even exceptional investigators get overwhelmed, take leave, or move on. And the next person assigned to a case may not have the same experience.

If the consistency of your investigations depends on who happens to be assigned, you may have more than a staffing problem. You may have a process problem.

One investigator sends a detailed notice. Another sends almost nothing.

One report explains the evidence and policy analysis before reaching a finding. Another goes straight to the conclusion.

That does not necessarily mean either investigator lacks skill. It may mean your team does not have a clear, shared way of doing the work.

This is not Investigations 101. This is infrastructure building.

Whether you are developing a new investigation function or strengthening one that is already doing good work, we look closely at the policies, protocols, decisions, and handoffs behind your investigations. This is not a lecture on protected categories. It is a working engagement focused on how your team actually operates and what needs to be clearer, more consistent, or more defensible.

We do not arrive with a rigid template and tell you to use it. We bring legal and practical investigative experience, and you bring knowledge of your organization, its culture, resources, and responsibilities. Within the requirements that apply to your organization, we work through the options together and make decisions you can implement.

The questions we work through

  • Intake and triage

    Who receives complaints, determines what needs investigation, assigns cases, and tracks the process?

  • Party notice and procedural fairness

    When and how are parties notified? What information and opportunities to respond should your process provide?

  • Investigation protocols

    How are allegations framed, cases scoped, evidence documented, and credibility questions addressed?

  • Roles and handoffs

    Where do the investigator, HR, Legal, Compliance, reviewers, and decision-makers enter and leave the process?

  • Reporting and quality review

    What belongs in the final report? How should evidence, policy analysis, and findings be organized and reviewed?

  • Continuity

    What templates, checkpoints, timelines, and documentation standards help the process hold up through turnover and changing caseloads?

What the Intensive looks like

  1. First, we map the current state. We review the relevant policies and materials, talk through actual workflows, and identify where practice and written expectations may not align.

  2. Then we make the important decisions together. We consider the legal and practical tradeoffs, decide which gaps matter most, and determine what a workable approach looks like for your organization.

  3. Finally, we turn those decisions into something your team can use.

Depending on the agreed scope, that may include:

  • A readiness assessment with prioritized recommendations.
  • Clearer intake, triage, assignment, review, and handoff workflows.
  • Draft investigation protocols or proposed policy and procedure revisions.
  • Standards or templates for party notices, report content, and quality review.
  • An implementation roadmap identifying what to change first and who owns the next steps.

Some organizations need a focused working session. Others need a deeper, multi-day engagement. The scope depends on what you are building, what already works, and how much change you want to make.

The goal is not to make every investigator work identically. It is to make sound investigative practices easier to apply, teach, review, and sustain, even when your team changes.

How CLEAR fits into both services

We use the CLEAR By-the-Book™ Investigation Framework for the cases we handle. It is also the starting point when we help internal teams decide how their own investigations should be structured.

  1. Capture the Complaint.

  2. Launch the Strategy.

  3. Evaluate the Evidence.

  4. Analyze Impartially.

  5. Report with Clarity.

CLEAR does not replace judgment. It gives investigators a consistent way to work through each stage of a case while leaving room for the facts, policies, and people involved.

INVEXTRA™: CLEAR beyond the engagement

Investigation expertise, amplified.

You may not need an outside investigator for every case. That does not mean you have to work without a framework.

INVEXTRA™ is an AI-enhanced support tool for protected-class investigators, built around the CLEAR By-the-Book™ investigation framework. It is designed to help investigators apply CLEAR as they work: clarifying allegations, planning interviews and evidence collection, identifying gaps, examining their reasoning, and writing clearer reports.

It does not investigate cases or make findings for you. The professional judgment and responsibility stay with the investigator.
